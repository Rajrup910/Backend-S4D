RESEARCH FLOW: Problem 02: Class Imbalance → CNN Baselines (ResNet, ConvNeXt, DenseNet, EfficientNet) → Accuracy Flaw → Clinical Metric Shift →
WHY DID WE DO THIS? Standard computer vision reports raw top-1 accuracy. In skin disease screening, 67% of patients present with benign moles. Primary Selection Metric

Macro-Averaged F1

Calculates the unweighted harmonic mean of Precision and Recall across all 7 classes equally:

Macro-F1 = (1/7) * Σ [2 * P_c * R_c / (P_c + R_c)]
Forces models to perform on rare classes (DF, VASC, AKIEC).
Clinical Recall Metric

Balanced Accuracy

Computes the arithmetic mean of sensitivity (recall) across each diagnostic category:

Bal-Acc = (1/7) * Σ Recall_c
Equal weight per class regardless of lesion sample count.
Patient Safety Gate

Escalation Sensitivity

Measures the recall on all biopsy-requiring malignant and premalignant lesions:

Sens_E = TP_E / (TP_E + FN_E), E = {mel, bcc, akiec}
N = 290 serious cases in held-out test split.

Statistical Significance & Rigorous Testing

To guarantee that model comparisons reflect true superiority rather than stochastic variance, all claims are governed by pre-registered hypothesis tests:

Uncorrected: p = 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ | Holm-corrected: p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴
CRITICAL DISTINCTION: McNemar evaluates paired classification disagreement on the contingency table; it is not a direct hypothesis test of Macro-F1.
Lesion-Grouped Bootstrap Intervals B = 1,000 resamples

DeLong's algorithm is applied for non-parametric ROC comparison; the step-down Holm-Bonferroni correction guards against false discovery across multiple testing families.

Mechanistic Explanation: Why is the ensemble underconfident when individual networks are overconfident? Soft-voting averages 6 models that often agree on the winning class but disperse their secondary probability mass across different runner-up classes. Why Dirichlet Calibration Wins: Unlike 1-parameter Temperature Scaling ($p_i = \operatorname{softmax}(z_i / T)$), Dirichlet calibration parameterizes an affine transformation across the full log-odds simplex ($W \ln p + b$), learning cross-class correlation and non-uniform class distortions.

Why Did Calibration Harm Cancer Screening?

Dirichlet calibration learns an affine transformation on the predicted log-odds simplex to align probabilities with empirical class frequencies. Because benign nevi account for 67% of cases and malignant lesions account for only ~19.5%, the calibrator penalizes rare-class probability mass.

For borderline lesions where the uncalibrated ensemble gave a slight melanoma probability (e.g., $P( ext{mel}) = 0.38, P( ext{nv}) = 0.35$), calibration shifts the probability toward the dominant prior, resulting in $P( ext{nv}) > P( ext{mel})$. Consequently, the standard argmax decision flips from melanoma to benign nevus.

Statistical Significance: Sensitivity drop Δ = -0.0552 is statistically significant (p = 0.0074, Paired McNemar Test).
Since calibration cannot ensure screening safety, we turned to Selective Classification (Abstention).

The Mechanism:
Margin ≠ Correctness
Wrong predictions in young patients have wide margins.

Scientific Analysis: Margin-based abstention tests the gap between the top prediction and the runner-up: $\operatorname{margin}(x) = p_{(1)}(x) - p_{(2)}(x)$. It assumes that errors are accompanied by uncertainty. In young patients, however, the model is confidently wrong: when a 28-year-old patient presents with melanoma, the model predicts benign nevus with 85%+ probability. Because the margin is large, the case bypasses the abstention filter and is never referred!

This fatal failure forced us to conduct an exhaustive demographic subgroup audit.

How Prior Skew Imprints on Decision Boundaries

When trained with Cross-Entropy loss under standard empirical risk minimization, CNNs implicitly learn the marginal prior:

P(Y = c | x) ∝ P(x | Y = c) · P(Y = c)

In dermoscopy, early melanoma in young adults frequently lacks classical ABCDE features (such as severe asymmetry or deep pigmentation) and closely mimics benign nevi.

Because the model has seen 20 benign moles for every 1 cancer in patients under 40, the posterior probability is overwhelmed by the prior: $P(\text{nv}) \gg P(\text{mel})$. As a result, the network systematically misclassifies young melanomas as benign moles with high confidence.

Problem & Question

Observed Data / Evidence

Ensemble is underconfident! Mean confidence = 0.7048, Accuracy = 0.8609, Signed Gap = -0.156, ECE = 0.1575. Soft-voting models that disagree on runner-up class depresses top confidence.

Aggregate ECE improved dramatically, BUT clinical screening sensitivity collapsed: 0.7862 → 0.7310! Missed serious cancers jumped from 62 to 78.

Problem & Question

Observed Data / Evidence

Sensitivity drop: 0.7862 → 0.7310 (-0.0552, paired McNemar $p = 0.0074$). Serious cancers missed: 62 → 78. Dirichlet maps probabilities toward empirical base rates, depressing rare cancer probabilities below the argmax decision threshold.

Problem 05: Selective Abstention & The Discovery of Confident Young Errors

Problem & Question

Can confidence/margin-based abstention safely refer ambiguous lesions to specialists and rescue missed cancers?

Observed Data / Evidence

The Discovery

Under-40 malignant errors are confidently wrong. Margin measures closeness to runner-up; when the model is confident in a benign nv prediction, margin is huge and abstention fails entirely.

Problem 06: Conformal Prediction — Marginal Collapse vs Group Conditioning

Problem & Question

Observed Data / Evidence

Marginal coverage reaches 90.4% overall, but collapses to 74.8% on malignant lesions (63 false reassurances) and 23.8% on young patients!

Problem 07 & 08: Under-40 Hidden Demographic Stratification & The 7.33× Prior Skew

The Empirical Shock

Under-40 escalation sensitivity: 0.143 (only 3 of 21 caught, 95% CI [0.030, 0.363]) vs 60+: 0.764 (152 of 199 caught). Disparity = -0.621 (Holm p < 0.001)!

Root-Cause Investigation

Audited training set priors: Under-40 escalating prior is 4.85% (64 / 1,319) vs 60+ prior is 35.55% (893 / 2,512) — a 7.33× demographic skew in training distribution!

Decision Taken

Formulate the Age-Conditional Escalation Rule: $\hat{y}(x) = \operatorname{argmax}_c [ p_c(x) + \lambda_b(x) \cdot \mathbf{1}[c \in \mathcal{E}] ]$ to inject demographic prior compensation.

Open Scientific Question

Does this rule solve the blind spot? And do these frozen parameters transport to independent hospital cohorts?

7.33× Training Prior Skew: 4.85% (<40) vs 35.55% (60+)

Problem 09: Age Rule Mitigation, External Transport, & The Causal Boundary

HAM10000 Test Pass Result

Frozen $\lambda$: $\lambda_{<40}=0.26, \lambda_{40-59}=0.74, \lambda_{60+}=0.33$. Overall sensitivity rises 0.731 → 0.831; under-40 sensitivity improves 0.143 → 0.238 (3/21 → 5/21 caught). Trade-off: NNB @ 3% rises from 3.0 to 6.2. Conclusion: MITIGATED, NOT CLOSED.

External Cohorts (16,991 Images)
