\begin{abstract}
Aggregate discrimination metrics on dermoscopic benchmarks have saturated: the gap between
our weakest and strongest backbone ($0.7058$ versus $0.7459$ Macro-F1) is not significant,
and only ensembling clears a paired test. Meanwhile the properties that decide whether such a
system can be placed in a clinical pathway --- calibration, conformal safety, subgroup
reliability --- are rarely measured per subgroup. We measure them on HAM10000 ($10{,}015$ images,
$7{,}470$ lesions, lesion-grouped split, one pre-registered test pass) and find an
age-stratified blind spot: escalation sensitivity is $0.143$ $[0.030, 0.363]$ in patients
under~$40$ against $0.764$ $[0.683, 0.836]$ at $60+$ (difference $-0.621$, Holm $p<0.001$),
with eighteen escalating lesions classified as ordinary nevi. Both standard safety nets fail
against it: margin-based abstention defers only $11\%$ of those misses because the errors are
confidently wrong, and marginal conformal coverage reads $90.1\%$ while under-$40$ malignant
coverage is $0.238$. Two subgroup-conditional repairs narrow the gap without closing it:
bipartite equalised conformal calibration lifts under-$40$ malignant coverage from $0.238$ to
$0.952$, and a frozen one-parameter age rule raises all-ages escalation sensitivity from
$0.731$ to $0.831$ at NNB $3.0\rightarrow6.2$ ($\pi=0.03$), though under~$40$ it reaches
only $0.238$ $[0.082, 0.472]$ --- the blind spot is mitigated, not closed. Carried frozen to
$14{,}885$ lesions across two further dermoscopic centres (nothing re-fitted), the operating
point transfers while both pre-registered mechanistic claims fail: the explanation does not
transport, and one fails backwards.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utomated} classification of dermoscopic images has been among the most
visible successes of deep learning in medicine. Since the demonstration that a convolutional
network could match board-certified dermatologists on biopsy-proven lesion
classification~\cite{esteva2017}, the field has converged on a small number of public
benchmarks --- principally the HAM10000 collection~\cite{tschandl2018ham10000} and the ISIC
challenge series~\cite{codella2018isic} --- and on a reporting convention centred on
aggregate discrimination: accuracy, macro-averaged F1, and area under the ROC curve.

That convention has outlived its usefulness. On HAM10000 the marginal returns to
architecture are now small and, as we show, statistically unresolvable at the size of a
standard held-out split: the gap between the weakest and strongest backbone we train
($0.7058$ versus $0.7459$ Macro-F1) is not significant under a paired test. Meanwhile the
properties that decide whether such a system can be safely integrated into a clinical pathway
are usually left unmeasured: calibration, selective deferral, clinically targeted conformal
validity, and subgroup reliability. Crucially, these dimensions address orthogonal failure
modes: calibration evaluates whether confidences reflect true probabilities; selective prediction
identifies when to defer; conformal inference constructs set-valued predictions with statistical
validity; and subgroup auditing checks demographic parity. Evaluated only in aggregate, each
mechanism appears reassuring. In a dataset where two thirds of images are benign nevi, however,
aggregate metrics are purchased from the majority, leaving the vulnerable minority unprotected.
This paper is organised around the principle that \emph{deployability is an empirical
subpopulation property}, that measuring it changes which design choices matter, and that
measuring it \emph{in aggregate} conceals catastrophic failure modes.

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper shifts the focus from architectural tuning on public benchmarks to an empirical
evaluation of medical AI safety nets under subgroup heterogeneity and distribution shift:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A subgroup safety blind spot, and the failure of both standard safety nets
against it.} We document an age-stratified escalation-sensitivity collapse that no aggregate
metric reveals ($0.143$ under~$40$ versus $0.764$ at $60+$), characterise it as a
\emph{confidently wrong} failure driven by the training prior, and show that both selective
classification and marginal conformal prediction fail in the affected subgroup
(Secs.~\ref{sec:results-fairness},~\ref{sec:results-conformal}).
\item \textbf{Subgroup-conditional mitigations, priced in clinical cost.} We supply two
repairs --- bipartite equalised conformal calibration and a frozen one-parameter
age-conditional decision rule --- and price the latter in Number Needed to Biopsy at a stated
reference prevalence ($\pi=0.03$), with the measured caveat that the two mechanisms are not
independent in the band that matters most
(Secs.~\ref{sec:results-conformal},~\ref{sec:results-utility}).
\item \textbf{Multi-centre replication and a transportability paradox.} We carry the frozen
system and its mitigation to two further dermoscopic centres ($14{,}885$ lesions, nothing
re-fitted) and report the result against its own pre-registered contingency: the operating
point transfers with zero re-tuning while the causal prior-skew mechanism not only fails to
replicate but fails in the reverse direction (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-battery}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Backbones and the imbalance they hide.} Convolutional baselines on HAM10000 and ISIC
are well established~\cite{esteva2017,codella2018isic,tschandl2018ham10000}, and the
architectural frontier has moved through residual~\cite{he2016resnet},
densely-connected~\cite{huang2017densenet}, compound-scaled~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} and
modernised-convolutional~\cite{liu2022convnext} designs, and more recently to hierarchical
Vision Transformers~\cite{liu2022swinv2,tu2022maxvit}. Reported gains between these families
on a single benchmark split are typically one to three F1 points --- smaller, as our
confidence intervals show, than the sampling variation of the split itself. HAM10000 is
dominated by melanocytic nevi ($\sim67\%$), so accuracy is an actively misleading selection
criterion; the standard remedies are re-weighting by effective number of
samples~\cite{cui2019classbalanced}, margin-based losses such as LDAM-DRW~\cite{cao2019ldam},
asymmetric focal variants~\cite{ridnik2021asl}, and post-hoc logit adjustment by the label
prior~\cite{menon2021logitadjust,saerens2002adjusting}. We find none of these improves on a
plain cross-entropy baseline once effective-number weighting is already applied
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ladder}).

\textbf{Calibration, uncertainty and abstention.} Modern single networks are systematically
overconfident~\cite{guo2017calibration}; temperature scaling is the standard one-parameter
remedy, and Dirichlet calibration~\cite{kull2019dirichlet} generalises it to full
class-conditional recalibration. Our soft-vote ensemble is miscalibrated in the
\emph{opposite} direction: mean confidence $0.7048$ against accuracy $0.8609$ (signed gap
$-0.156$), because averaging six probability vectors that disagree about the runner-up class
pulls the maximum probability down. This is why a one-parameter temperature is the wrong
remedy here, and Dirichlet calibration --- which cuts test ECE from $0.1575$ to $0.0206$ ---
is used throughout instead. Group-wise notions such as
multicalibration~\cite{hebertjohnson2018multicalibration} go further, requiring the property
to hold within subpopulations rather than on average; our per-band residuals, which disagree
in sign across age bands even after a global Dirichlet map, are a direct empirical argument
for one (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-conformal}). Selective classification has a long
history~\cite{elyaniv2010selective,geifman2017selectivenet}; we use a probability-margin
abstention gate throughout.

\textbf{Hidden stratification and conformal coverage.} Split conformal
prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic} converts non-conformity scores into valid set-valued
predictions with finite-sample coverage. LAC~\cite{sadinle2019lac} minimises set size;
APS~\cite{romano2020aps} and RAPS~\cite{angelopoulos2021raps} trade size for conditional
validity. The distinction between marginal and class-conditional (Mondrian)
coverage~\cite{vovk2012conditional} is, in our reading, under-emphasised in medical imaging,
where it is precisely the clinically relevant distinction; conditioning on a clinically
salient attribute rather than on the label --- equalised
coverage~\cite{romano2020equalized} --- is the construction our bipartite (age band
$\times$ escalation requirement) calibrator instantiates. Oakden-Rayner \emph{et
al.}~\cite{oakdenrayner2020hidden} define \emph{hidden stratification} as a clinically
meaningful subclass systematically mishandled while aggregate metrics stay healthy;
Seyyed-Kalantari \emph{et al.}~\cite{seyyedkalantari2021underdiagnosis} report the closest
prior result, underdiagnosis across patient groups in chest radiography. The mechanism here is
a shortcut in the sense of Geirhos \emph{et al.}~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}: ``young patient,
therefore nevus'', sourced in the training prior we report in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-fairness}. Our delta over this lineage is to measure the mechanism with a
threshold-free within-band statistic, to show the safety mechanisms themselves are inoperative
in the affected subgroup, and to price a mitigation --- a group-specific threshold in the
sense of Hardt \emph{et al.}~\cite{hardt2016equality} --- in Number Needed to Biopsy rather
than as an accuracy delta.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data and cohorts}
All experiments use HAM10000~\cite{tschandl2018ham10000}: $10{,}015$ dermoscopic images of
$7{,}470$ distinct lesions across seven diagnostic categories. Because the collection contains
multiple images of the same lesion, an image-level split leaks; we therefore split by
\texttt{lesion\_id} at a $70/15/15$ ratio, stratified by diagnosis, giving $6{,}981$ training
images ($5{,}229$ lesions), $1{,}532$ validation images ($1{,}120$ lesions) and $1{,}502$ test
images ($1{,}121$ lesions), with a machine-checked assertion that no lesion identifier appears
in more than one split. For the pre-registered same-modality replication of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-battery} we use two further dermoscopic centres from
ISIC-2019~\cite{combalia2019bcn20000,codella2018isic}, BCN-20000 and MSKCC, held in a
lesion-grouped split of $14{,}885$ records. Squamous-cell carcinoma is excluded by
pre-registration, since it has no HAM10000 counterpart and the frozen head cannot emit it;
that removes $431$ BCN-20000 images and leaves $11{,}982$ against MSKCC's $2{,}903$. MSKCC's
label space holds only \textsc{nv}, \textsc{mel} and \textsc{bkl}, so seven-class Macro-F1 is
undefined there. Both cohorts are scored by the same six frozen HAM10000-only checkpoints under
the same test-time augmentation, ensemble, calibrator and age-conditional rule described below;
no parameter anywhere in this paper is fitted on BCN-20000 or MSKCC.

\subsection{Backbones, ensembling and calibration}
Six ImageNet-pretrained CNN backbones --- ResNet-50~\cite{he2016resnet},
DenseNet-121~\cite{huang2017densenet}, EfficientNet-B0 and
-B3~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}, ConvNeXt-Tiny and -Small~\cite{liu2022convnext} --- are
fine-tuned identically under effective-number class weighting~\cite{cui2019classbalanced}, a
two-stage schedule and conservative augmentation; two transformer backbones
(SwinV2-Tiny~\cite{liu2022swinv2}, MaxViT-Tiny~\cite{tu2022maxvit}) and two alternative losses
(LDAM-DRW~\cite{cao2019ldam}, ASL~\cite{ridnik2021asl}) are trained under the same protocol.
The deployed ensemble is a uniform arithmetic soft-vote over the six CNN members' probability
vectors, chosen over rank averaging, Nelder--Mead weighting, Caruana greedy
selection~\cite{caruana2004ensemble} and ridge stacking, all fitted out-of-fold on validation.
Test-time augmentation pools $24$ deterministic dihedral views per image using only
evaluation-time transforms. Four calibrators were fitted on validation and compared by
validation ECE --- none, temperature scaling, matrix scaling, and Dirichlet
calibration~\cite{kull2019dirichlet} with $L_2$ regularisation; Dirichlet wins and is used for
all downstream stages.

\subsection{Out-of-fold protocol, abstention and conformal sets}
\label{sec:oof}
Fitting every downstream component on the $1{,}532$-image validation split makes it do six jobs
at once and starves rare classes of calibration support. We therefore retrain the six backbones
under $5$-fold \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} cross-validation (lesion-grouped, seed $42$) to
obtain out-of-fold (OOF) predictions covering the entire training split, which raises rare-class
yield to $71$ \textsc{df} and $99$ \textsc{vasc} rows (against $24$ and $22$ in validation) and
to $64$ escalating under-$40$ cases (against $22$), at the cost of a stacking mismatch we
measure but do not remove: OOF row $i$ is scored by a model trained on $80\%$ of the data,
whereas every deployed prediction comes from a model trained on all of it.

Selective prediction uses the top-two margin as the uncertainty score, selected by validation
area under the risk--coverage curve over seven candidates; a case is deferred when its score
exceeds the validation quantile at the target abstention rate. Conformal sets use split
conformal with three score functions --- LAC~\cite{sadinle2019lac}, APS~\cite{romano2020aps},
RAPS~\cite{angelopoulos2021raps} --- under three calibration schemes: marginal, Mondrian
class-conditional~\cite{vovk2012conditional}, and \emph{bipartite}, which groups calibration
points by the pair (age band, escalation requirement) and is an instance of equalised
coverage~\cite{romano2020equalized}. Calibrating conformal quantiles on the OOF rows instead of
the validation split raises the rarest class's calibration count from $11$ to $45$ and the
total calibration pool from $762$ to $3{,}468$ rows, eliminating every degenerate
class-conditional cell at $\alpha=0.05$ --- at the cost of the exact finite-sample guarantee,
since calibration and test scores then come from different score functions (five fold models
versus the full-train ensemble); we audit OOF-calibrated coverage empirically against nominal
$1-\alpha$ and never assert it as guaranteed.

Two clinical-utility quantities are used throughout and defined here. \emph{Number Needed to
Biopsy} (NNB) is $(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP})/\mathrm{TP}$, re-weighted to a stated reference
prevalence $\pi$ by importance-weighting the benign cases,
\begin{equation}
w = \frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\cdot\frac{p}{1-p}, \qquad
\mathrm{NNB}_{\pi} = 1 + w\,\frac{\mathrm{FP}}{\mathrm{TP}},
\label{eq:nnb}
\end{equation}
with $p$ the cohort's observed escalating prevalence; we use $\pi=0.03$ throughout. \emph{False
Reassurance Rate} (FRR) is the share of truly escalating lesions whose conformal set contains
no escalating class at all,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FRR} = \frac{|\{i : y_i \in \mathcal{E},\ \mathcal{C}(x_i)\cap\mathcal{E}=\emptyset\}|}
                    {|\{i : y_i \in \mathcal{E}\}|},
\label{eq:frr}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{E}$ the escalating set $\escset$.

\subsection{The age-conditional escalation rule}
\label{sec:agerule}
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-fairness} shows that in the under-$40$ band the escalating-class
probability mass carries usable signal that the $\arg\max$ rule discards. The mitigation is
therefore a decision rule, not a retrained model, kept to one parameter per age band $b(x)$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}(x) \;=\; \arg\max_{c}\;\bigl(p_c(x) + \lambda_{b(x)}\,\mathbf{1}[c \in \mathcal{E}]\bigr),
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
equivalent to per-class thresholds $\theta_c=-\lambda_b$ on the escalating classes and $0$
elsewhere --- the group-specific threshold construction of Hardt \emph{et
al.}~\cite{hardt2016equality}, applied to an age band. Each $\lambda_b$ is chosen by exhaustive
grid search to minimise expected clinical cost subject to a per-band escalation-specificity
floor of $0.85$; a band with fewer than $30$ true escalating cases falls back to the pooled
value, which is why the rule is fitted on OOF and nowhere else --- validation has $22$
escalating under-$40$ cases and OOF has $64$. Fitting uses cross-fitted Dirichlet-calibrated
OOF probabilities, so the calibrator has not seen the rows its own $\lambda$ is tuned on. The
frozen values are $\lambda_{<40}=0.26$ (bootstrap CI touching zero, $[0.00,0.61]$),
$\lambda_{40\text{--}59}=0.74$, $\lambda_{60+}=0.33$.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
\label{sec:frozen}
A proportion with fewer than $30$ events in the numerator takes the exact Clopper--Pearson
interval; everything else takes a lesion-grouped bootstrap percentile interval, because the
$1{,}502$ test images sit on $1{,}121$ lesions and resampling images would treat correlated
views as independent evidence. Paired comparisons that share the $N=1502$ denominator use
McNemar's test~\cite{dietterich1998}; per-class discrimination uses DeLong's
test~\cite{delong1988} with the fast structural-component algorithm~\cite{sun2014fastdelong}.
Seven families are declared in advance and Holm--Bonferroni corrected within family: four
confirmatory, whose adjusted $p$-values are the ones quoted, and three exploratory, reported as
intervals only. The confirmatory families are the $42$ DeLong tests, the six ladder McNemar
tests, the single pre-specified age-band comparison, and the two rungs of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ladder} evaluated beyond A7. Every reported test result derives from
frozen artifact sets --- $34$ per-image prediction matrices, hashed before the statistical
analysis was written --- and every new quantity this round added (two ladder rungs, the age
rule, NNB, FRR, bipartite coverage, intersectional cells) was named in a pre-registered
$19$-item analysis plan \emph{before} the test split was read, then emitted by a single runner
that raises on any identifier the plan does not contain. The pass reported here ran in two
stages with no repeat and no stated rerun reason; as a reproduction check, rung A7 recomputed
inside that pass matched its frozen value to zero drift. Reporting completeness against the
$44$-item CLAIM 2024 checklist~\cite{tejani2024claim} is $33$ items met, $7$ partial, $2$ not
met and $2$ not applicable, with every shortfall named in the project repository rather than
left blank.

\subsection{What does benchmark discrimination establish? The ablation ladder}
\label{sec:results-ladder}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_edited} reports the consolidated ladder. \textbf{Architecture barely
matters.} ConvNeXt-Tiny ($0.7459$, CI $[0.6928, 0.7806]$) improves on ResNet-50 ($0.7058$, CI

and McNemar against the best single model is decisive ($\chi^2=17.20$,
$p=3.4\times10^{-5}$, $85$ cases correct only for the ensemble against $38$ only for
ConvNeXt-Tiny); Holm correction inside the six-member ladder family leaves this comparison
significant at $p=2.0\times10^{-4}$ and every other comparison non-significant. After Holm
correction across all $42$ DeLong tests, exactly one per-class AUC comparison survives: the
ensemble improves \textsc{bkl} AUC from $0.9195$ to $0.9572$ ($z=3.61$, Holm $p=0.013$). The
McNemar result and the Macro-F1 bootstrap interval disagree in appearance because they answer
different questions --- McNemar tests raw correct/incorrect outcomes, dominated by the
$1{,}004$ \textsc{nv} images, while Macro-F1 weights all seven classes equally and inherits the
wide sampling variance of the rare classes. TTA adds $+0.014$ ($0.7859$) and Dirichlet
calibration a further $+0.019$ ($0.8047$), neither individually significant. Margin abstention
(Block B) trades coverage for retained quality: requested deferral rates of $5/10/15/20\%$
yield realised test coverages of $0.954/0.892/0.836/0.779$ --- deferring $10.8\%$ of cases
raises retained Macro-F1 to $0.8577$ on the retained $1{,}340$-image subset, and deferring
$22.1\%$ raises it to $0.8957$ on the retained $1{,}170$-image subset while cutting missed
serious cases to $36$.

\label{sec:results-exhaustion}
\textbf{Exhaustion: five further levers, five negative results.} Beyond rung A7 we tested five
combination levers under lesion-grouped cross-fitting or a pre-registered single test pass, and
none beats it. An eight-member vote adding both transformers is a wash ($0.7981$ against
$0.7986$ for the six-CNN vote); Caruana greedy selection~\cite{caruana2004ensemble} over eight
members overfits ($0.7640$/$0.7839$ against $0.7945$); tempered prior correction and per-class
Macro-F1 offsets are both inert or noisy. The two pre-registered rungs beyond A7 (Block C) are
also negative: OOF-fitted Dirichlet calibration (A7-oof, raw Macro-F1 $0.7871$ $[0.7315,
0.8295]$) costs $-0.0176$ $[-0.0451,+0.0054]$, Holm $p=0.242$ against A7, buying calibration
sample size rather than accuracy; a $30$-member fold-bagged ensemble (A8, raw Macro-F1
$0.7810$ $[0.7207, 0.8258]$), the one lever with a real mechanism behind it, costs $-0.0237$
$[-0.0625,+0.0043]$, Holm $p=0.242$, though it is under-sharpened by construction (it
reuses the six-member OOF Dirichlet map) and so is a lower bound on fold-bagging rather than a
refutation. Macro-F1 $0.8047$ is the ceiling of these checkpoints under every post-hoc
recombination we could find; ridge stacking would have produced our best test number
($0.7815$) but the \emph{worst} out-of-fold validation score ($0.7583$ against $0.7911$ for the
deployed vote), so we report it as a negative result rather than select it on the test set.

Fig.~\ref{fig:conformal} shows the per-class coverage profile behind these numbers. Aggregate ECE conceals structure that matters for calibration and for coverage alike. Sliced by
age band on the deployed TTA ensemble, the per-band ECE spread is $0.084$ before Dirichlet
calibration and $0.029$ after --- narrowed, not closed --- and out-of-fold, the under-$40$ band
is simultaneously the most ``accurate'' ($0.930$, dominated by easy nevi) and the most
under-confident of the three ($-0.229$ against $-0.190$ and $-0.119$); after calibration the
$60+$ band is pushed into over-confidence (val $+0.030$, OOF $+0.041$) while the under-$40$
band remains under-confident (OOF $-0.027$). One global affine map cannot be simultaneously
correct for a band it must sharpen and a band it must soften.

Table~\ref{tab:conformal_edited} shows the coverage consequence. At $\alpha=0.10$, LAC under
marginal calibration attains $90.1\%$ overall coverage --- the guarantee holds exactly as
advertised --- while covering only $73.4\%$ of genuinely malignant lesions and just $23.8\%$
of malignant lesions in patients under $40$, against nominal $0.90$. Mondrian
class-conditional calibration repairs the aggregate malignant coverage to $0.886$--$0.903$ but
lifts the under-$40$ malignant figure only to $0.571$--$0.667$: conditioning on the label
fixes the label imbalance and leaves the patient imbalance untouched. Calibrating on the pair
(age band, escalation requirement) --- the bipartite scheme --- closes it, to $0.952$ (LAC)
and $0.857$ (RAPS) at $\alpha=0.10$, with aggregate serious coverage at or above the
class-conditional value and a mean set size that is smaller, not larger. Out-of-fold
calibration of these conformal quantiles raises the rarest class's calibration count from $11$
to $45$ and the pool from $762$ to $3{,}468$ rows, eliminating every degenerate cell at
$\alpha=0.05$, at the cost of the exact finite-sample guarantee stated in
Sec.~\ref{sec:oof}.

\textbf{False reassurance (Eq.~\eqref{eq:frr}) is the endpoint we report, and we report the
$\alpha$ that bounds it
rather than one chosen in advance.} Under marginal calibration FRR reaches $0.228$ at
$\alpha=0.10$; RAPS with bipartite calibration at $\alpha=0.05$ is the best configuration
measured, FRR $0.0138$ $[0.0038, 0.0349]$ with serious coverage $0.955$ and mean set size
$1.87$. Bounding FRR below $0.05$ needs $\alpha=0.05$ (set size $1.87$), below $0.02$ needs
$\alpha=0.02$ (set size $3.25$), and \textbf{no $\alpha$ in our grid bounds FRR below $0.01$}
under any of the eighteen configurations we evaluated --- reported as unreachable rather than
quietly clipped.

\subsection{Does reliability persist across patient subgroups? An age-stratified blind spot}
\label{sec:results-fairness}

\input{tables_edited/table_agegap_edited.tex}

Every aggregate number reported so far is consistent with a competent system; stratifying by
patient age shows it is not, uniformly. On test, patients aged $60$ and over have $76.4\%$ of
escalating lesions caught and the $40$--$59$ band $81.4\%$; patients under $40$ have $14.3\%$
--- three of twenty-one --- with eighteen lesions requiring escalation, most of them
melanomas, classified as ordinary nevi. The single pre-specified confirmatory comparison,
under $40$ against $60+$, gives a difference of $-0.621$ $[-0.786,-0.386]$, Holm $p<0.001$. The
point estimate $0.143$ rests on twenty-one positives and should never be quoted bare; the same
quantity is $0.545$ on validation and $0.547$ out-of-fold, and the pre-specified confirmatory
comparison on the better-powered out-of-fold split gives $-0.187$ $[-0.370,-0.016]$, Holm
$p=0.036$ --- the same sign and significance at a smaller magnitude. What is stable across all
three splits is the ordering: under-$40$ is the worst band every time.

\textbf{The mechanism is both a decision rule and lost information.} A threshold-free
diagnostic asks how well the escalating-class probability mass $\sum_{c\in\mathcal{E}}p_c(x)$
ranks truly escalating lesions above benign ones within a band, using no threshold and no
calibration. On test the under-$40$ within-band AUC is $0.810$, against $0.975$ at $40$--$59$
and $0.933$ at $60+$ on the same split, where under-$40$ also ranks worst rather than
comparably. The probability vector still ranks escalating lesions under $40$ far above chance,
so a decision rule can recover part of the gap, but its ranking in that band is genuinely
weaker, so no threshold recovers all of it. \textbf{The source is the training prior.} In the
training split, $4.9\%$ of lesions in patients under $40$ require escalation, against $35.5\%$
at $60+$; a model trained to minimise a class-weighted loss over this distribution learns that
a pigmented lesion on a young patient is a nevus, correctly with respect to the training
objective and catastrophically with respect to the clinical one.

\textbf{The gap compounds with sex, within the limits our power gates allow.} Slicing age band
$\times$ sex under the same gates (at least $30$ images and $10$ true escalating cases before a
sensitivity is reported), escalation sensitivity is $0.848$ $[0.681,0.949]$ for women aged
$40$--$59$ and $0.784$ $[0.618,0.902]$ for men in the same band, $0.697$ $[0.551,0.833]$ for
women at $60+$ and $0.797$ $[0.709,0.874]$ for men; the one reportable under-$40$ cell, women,
is $0.250$ $[0.055,0.572]$ on twelve escalating lesions. Across the reportable cells the
equalised-odds TPR gap is $0.598$, the demographic-parity gap $0.339$, and the referral-burden
gap $0.129$. Men under $40$ hold nine escalating lesions against our floor of ten, so it fails the gate by one case; we name it as suppressed rather than dropping it silently. Age is the
dominant axis here; we do not claim a resolved sex effect at these interval widths.

\subsection{Can subgroup-conditional rules mitigate the blind spot, and at what clinical cost?}
\label{sec:results-utility}

We apply the rule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda} at the $\lambda_b$ frozen from out-of-fold fitting,
on top of rung A7-oof, and read it once on test (Table~\ref{tab:agegap_edited}). Overall
escalation sensitivity rises from $0.731$ to $0.831$ and missed serious cases fall from $78$ to
$49$, at a referral rate rising from $0.178$ to $0.268$ and a Macro-F1 cost of $-0.038$
($0.7871\rightarrow0.7492$). The rule helps every band and helps the $40$--$59$ band most
dramatically ($0.814\rightarrow0.971$, referral $0.109\rightarrow0.234$) and the under-$40$
band least ($0.143\rightarrow0.238$, referral $0.028\rightarrow0.066$), exactly the ordering the
escalation-mass diagnostic predicted. At $60+$ the rule moves sensitivity $0.764\rightarrow0.844$
at a referral cost of $0.350\rightarrow0.423$. Read against a stated reference prevalence
$\pi=0.03$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nnb}), overall NNB moves from $3.0$ to $6.2$: roughly six lesions
escalated per escalation-warranting lesion found, against three before, for twenty-nine fewer
missed serious cases; by band, NNB moves $5.2\rightarrow8.1$ under $40$, $2.1\rightarrow5.9$ at
$40$--$59$, and $5.3\rightarrow7.6$ at $60+$. The range is wide at other reference prevalences
($7.1\rightarrow16.9$ at $\pi=0.01$, $2.2\rightarrow4.1$ at $\pi=0.05$), which is itself the
point: an NNB quoted without its prevalence is not a number a clinician can use. Under-$40$
escalation sensitivity remains poor in absolute terms after the intervention, and we do not
present this as a solved problem.

\textbf{Is it redundant with abstention? In the band it was built for, yes.} The orthogonality
check below uses the OOF-refit margin gate, which at a $10\%$ target retains $89.9\%$ of cases,
raising retained Macro-F1 from $0.7871$ to $0.8444$ and cutting missed serious cases from $78$
to $57$. Intersecting the escalating lesions plain $\arg\max$ misses with the cases each
mechanism rescues: under $40$, of $18$ misses the abstention gate defers $2$ and the age rule
catches the same $2$ --- a Jaccard overlap of $1.00$, the rule reaches no case abstention does
not already reach, in the very band it targets. At $40$--$59$ the two are complementary: of
$13$ misses, abstention defers $2$ and the rule catches $11$, overlapping on $2$, Jaccard
$0.18$. At $60+$ they largely coincide (Jaccard $0.83$: of $47$ misses, abstention defers $17$
and the rule catches $16$). Pooled, of $78$ missed escalating lesions abstention defers $21$
and the rule catches $29$, Jaccard $0.61$ --- an aggregate that conceals total redundancy in
the band that matters most. Per-band miss-rescue rates are $11.1\%$ under $40$, $15.4\%$ at
$40$--$59$ and $36.2\%$ at $60+$ ($26.9\%$ pooled); the abstention gate itself defers only
$6.2\%$ of under-$40$ cases against $17.3\%$ at $60+$, because confidence is highest precisely
where the errors are. This is a negative result inside the paper's own headline contribution,
and we report it as measured: sixteen confidently wrong under-$40$ misses are reached by
neither mechanism, a gap we return to in Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}.

system needs $3.0$ biopsies per malignancy found at $\pi=0.03$ and sends $373$ of Tier-1
lesions to discharge under calibrated triage.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Summary of empirical claims and non-claims}
To maintain rigorous claim discipline and prevent over-interpretation of retrospective benchmark results, we delineate what our evidence establishes versus what it disclaims:

\textbf{What the study establishes:}
(i)~\emph{Benchmark saturation:} returns to single architectures have saturated; only ensembling yields a certifiable gain ($+0.0259$ Macro-F1, Holm $p=2.0\times10^{-4}$, ceiling $0.8047$).
(ii)~\emph{Calibration separation:} Dirichlet recalibration cuts test ECE from $0.1575$ to $0.0206$ without altering discriminative rankings.
(iii)~\emph{Selective deferral failure:} standard margin abstention defers only $11\%$ of under-$40$ escalating misses because errors are confidently wrong.
(iv)~\emph{Marginal conformal failure:} marginal $90.1\%$ coverage masks serious-lesion coverage of $73.4\%$ and under-$40$ malignant coverage of $23.8\%$.
(v)~\emph{Subgroup blind spot:} escalation sensitivity collapses from $0.764$ ($60+$) to $0.143$ (under $40$, difference $-0.621$, Holm $p<0.001$).
(vi)~\emph{Priced mitigation:} a frozen age rule raises all-ages sensitivity ($0.731\to0.831$) at NNB $3.0\to6.2$ ($\pi=0.03$), but reaches only $0.238$ under $40$.
(vii)~\emph{Transportability separation:} on $14{,}885$ external lesions, the operating point transfers while the causal prior-skew explanation fails.

\textbf{What the study does not establish:}
(i)~\emph{No clinical readiness:} the system is not clinically validated and must not operate autonomously (under-$40$ sensitivity is $0.238$).
(ii)~\emph{No universal causal mechanism:} the age-prior skew hypothesis is non-universal; its ordering reversed across external centres.
(iii)~\emph{No universally optimal threshold:} the direction of the age rule transfers, but its exact scalar requires local site recalibration.
(iv)~\emph{No global benchmark invalidation:} findings demonstrate benchmark discrimination is insufficient evidence of deployment reliability, not that benchmark models are invalid.

\subsection{Aggregate conformal coverage conceals clinically relevant failure}
The conformal result generalises beyond this dataset. Any screening problem with a dominant
benign class allows a marginal coverage guarantee to be met almost entirely by the majority,
and the more imbalanced the problem, the more comprehensively the guarantee can be satisfied
while failing the patients it exists to protect. Our $\alpha=0.10$ LAC configuration is
formally valid, empirically on-target at $90.1\%$, and leaves roughly one malignant lesion in
four with a shortlist containing nothing that would prompt referral. Conditioning on the
label --- Mondrian class-conditional calibration --- restores aggregate malignant coverage and
still leaves malignant lesions in patients under~$40$ covered at $0.571$--$0.667$:
label-conditional validity is blind to patient-level structure. Conditioning jointly on age
band and escalation requirement, an instance of equalised
coverage~\cite{romano2020equalized}, raises that to $0.952$ at no cost in set size, because
six well-populated cells estimate their quantiles more sharply than seven cells two of which
are nearly empty. Medical applications of conformal prediction should report coverage
conditioned on the clinically positive set \emph{and} on the subgroups the system is meant to
serve, with the false reassurance rate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:frr}) as a primary reporting endpoint.

\subsection{Confidently wrong errors: why selective prediction fails}
The selective-classification literature rests on the premise that a model's uncertainty
correlates with its errors, and our aggregate risk--coverage curves support that premise. The
age analysis shows where it breaks: in the under-$40$ band the model's errors carry no
elevated uncertainty, and the abstention gate defers a \emph{smaller} fraction of that band
($6.2\%$) than of the $60+$ band ($17.3\%$), so the safety net thins out exactly where it is
needed. The probability vector does carry signal --- within-band escalation-mass AUC is
$0.810$ --- but every standard safety mechanism reads a summary of it that discards the
escalating-mass direction, which is where the residual signal lives. Our one-parameter rule
reads exactly that direction and recovers part of the gap ($0.143 \rightarrow 0.238$) where
abstention recovers almost none. Only part, though: the within-band AUC is also genuinely
lower under~$40$ than in the older bands, so some of the gap is lost ranking ability that no
decision rule reading this probability vector can recover.

\subsection{What exports is the intervention, not the explanation}
Carrying the frozen system to three centres separates whether the \emph{finding} generalises
from whether the \emph{fix} does. The fix generalises: a per-band $\lambda$ fitted once on
HAM10000 out-of-fold data raises under-$40$ escalation sensitivity at both external centres,
with nothing re-fitted. The explanation does not: neither pre-registered claim survived ---
escalation-mass AUC is not cohort-invariant (spread $0.105$), and under-$40$ sensitivity
tracked the training prior's age skew in reverse. The honest reading is that we have a
transportable \emph{operating point} and a non-transportable \emph{mechanism}. A
group-conditional threshold encodes a policy that survives a change of cohort in a way that
causal stories about why the base rate misleads do not. The corollary for deployment: the
\emph{direction} of the correction can be imported, but its magnitude cannot --- a
target-fitted sweep puts the under-$40$ optimum at $\lambda=0.12$ in one external centre and
$0.91$ in the other, against $0.26$ frozen from the source. A site adopting this rule should
inherit its form and re-fit its scalar on local data. That costs us a causal claim and buys a
deployable intervention --- a trade worth making explicit rather than papering over.

\subsection{Subgroup heterogeneity and delta over prior hidden-stratification work}
Oakden-Rayner \emph{et al.}~\cite{oakdenrayner2020hidden} named hidden stratification;
Seyyed-Kalantari \emph{et al.}~\cite{seyyedkalantari2021underdiagnosis} demonstrated
underdiagnosis across patient groups in chest radiography; the training prior we identify
makes this a shortcut in the sense of Geirhos \emph{et
al.}~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}. What we add, as a delta rather than a displacement, is three
things prior work did not measure: first, a threshold-free within-band statistic separating
``the representation cannot see it'' from ``the decision rule throws it away'', with the
finding that on these data it is both; second, that the safety mechanisms themselves ---
abstention, calibration, conformal coverage --- are inoperative in the affected subgroup,
which is a stronger statement than that the classifier is worse there; third, a mitigation
priced in clinical cost (Number Needed to Biopsy at a stated reference prevalence) rather than
as an accuracy delta~\cite{hardt2016equality}.

\subsection{Clinical positioning and non-autonomous evaluation}
We do not claim this system is deployable. Even after mitigation, under-$40$ escalation
sensitivity is $0.238$; this system must not run autonomously for young patients. The analysis
identifies a problem and prices a partial remedy, it does not solve it. The configuration we
would put forward for prospective evaluation under specialist oversight is the TTA ensemble
with Dirichlet calibration, the age-conditional rule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda}, a
probability-based abstention gate at roughly $10\%$ deferral, and RAPS conformal sets under
bipartite calibration as the output format --- a shortlist, not a diagnosis. The entire
evaluation is retrospective, on a curated cohort, so every absolute utility figure here is an
internal comparison rather than a clinical forecast.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Data: one training cohort, and a scope decision.} All training is on HAM10000, and the
same-modality replication of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-battery} is the external evidence this paper
relies on; BCN-20000 is the centre the frozen system transfers to worst and MSKCC's label space
holds only three of our seven classes. We scope this paper to same-modality dermoscopy because a
change of imaging modality confounds every explanation at once: a cross-modality probe on the
smartphone cohort PAD-UFES-20 ($2{,}106$ images, an inverted class prior of roughly $77\%$
escalating against HAM10000's $19\%$) was run under the identical frozen system, the ensemble
did not transfer (Macro-F1 $0.167$, below its own best individual member), and that result on
PAD-UFES-20~\cite{pacheco2020padufes} is reported in the project repository rather than here. On skin tone, this paper's three cohorts
--- HAM10000, BCN-20000 and MSKCC --- carry no Fitzpatrick labels, and an image-derived
individual-typology-angle proxy is documented elsewhere in this project as invalid on
dermoscopy (vignetting and erythema dominate the measured angle), so we report neither here; a
genuine Fitzpatrick slice exists only for the smartphone cohort scoped out above, so this paper
has nothing to say about skin tone within the dermoscopic cohorts it covers.

\textbf{Protocol: the out-of-fold move closes one problem and opens two.} Out-of-fold
calibration is fitted by models trained on $80\%$ of the data and applied to a model trained on
all of it, a stacking mismatch we measure but cannot remove; rung A7-oof is $0.018$ Macro-F1
below its validation-fitted counterpart on an earlier validation-only check. Out-of-fold
conformal calibration forfeits the exact finite-sample guarantee, because calibration and test
scores no longer come from one fixed score function; we audit its coverage empirically and
never assert it. Every test quantity in this paper was named before it was computed under the
frozen analysis plan and statistical protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:frozen}, executed as a single
test pass with no rerun reason recorded; that is the intended cost of the discipline, not an
oversight.

\textbf{The mitigation: right form, locally wrong magnitude, and not orthogonal where it
matters.} Each $\lambda_b$ minimises expected cost under a per-band specificity floor rather
than the cleaner target of equality of opportunity~\cite{hardt2016equality}; the under-$40$
$\lambda$ has a bootstrap interval touching zero, the least certain of the three. The external
centres sharpen the point: the frozen value helps everywhere, but a target-fitted sweep puts the
under-$40$ optimum at $0.12$ and $0.91$ in the two external cohorts against $0.26$ frozen from
the source, so a site adopting this rule should inherit its form and re-fit its scalar locally.
The two safety mechanisms are not independent where it counts: at the $10\%$ operating point
the age rule and the abstention gate rescue the same two of eighteen under-$40$ misses, and
sixteen confidently wrong under-$40$ misses are reached by neither; we have no proposal that
reaches them.

\textbf{Evidence quality: two attributions corrected.} The \emph{level} of Macro-F1 is dragged
down by \textsc{akiec} ($F_1=0.697$) and \textsc{mel} ($0.699$) --- the two lowest per-class
scores on test, one of them the clinically decisive class --- and not by \textsc{df} ($0.791$)
or \textsc{vasc} ($0.842$); the \emph{variance} is dominated by \textsc{df} and \textsc{vasc},
whose intervals are $0.389$ and $0.308$ wide on twenty and twenty-one images. Rare-class
scarcity widens the intervals; melanoma performance holds the point estimate down. Separately,
a Grad-CAM lesion-interior attribution check over all test images, using Tschandl's binary
lesion segmentations~\cite{tschandl2020segmentations}, finds $0.523$ of activation mass falling
inside the annotated lesion against a mean lesion area of $0.256$ of the frame; but
misclassified images score \emph{higher} on this fraction ($0.570$) than correct ones
($0.513$), because for a misclassified image the map~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} is taken with
respect to the \emph{predicted} class, so landing on the lesion is close to automatic. This is
a supporting check, not evidence of mechanism; the mechanistic claim in this paper is carried
by the threshold-free within-band escalation-mass AUC of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-fairness}
instead.

\textbf{Age is a proxy for something we do not observe.} We stratify by recorded patient age
because it is what the metadata contains; the underlying clinical structure --- lesion
presentation, nevus count, sun-exposure history --- is unobserved. The three-centre replication
makes this concrete: the same age label indexes a different case mix at every site, which is
one reason the mitigation transported and the mechanism did not.

\section{Conclusion}

We built a dermoscopic lesion classifier as an ablation ladder under a strict leak-free
protocol --- lesion-grouped splits, no downstream component fitted on test, out-of-fold
predictions where validation was doing too many jobs, and one pre-registered pass over the test
split --- and found that among the design choices evaluated, only ensembling produced a
statistically certifiable gain in Macro-F1; five further combination levers were all negative.
Macro-F1 $0.8047$ is the ceiling of these checkpoints, and we report that as a result rather
than a gap. The system's deployability properties moved substantially: calibration error fell
by a factor of seven, an age-conditional decision rule raised escalation sensitivity from
$0.731$ to $0.831$ at a Number Needed to Biopsy of $6.2$ at a $3\%$ reference prevalence, and
subgroup-conditional conformal calibration cut the false reassurance rate to $0.0138$
$[0.0038,0.0349]$. Three findings should outlast the specific numbers. Marginal conformal
coverage is satisfiable and misleading in any imbalanced screening problem, and class-conditional
coverage is not automatically the fix, because conditioning on the label leaves patient-level
structure untouched. A model can be confidently wrong about an entire patient subgroup ---
escalation sensitivity $0.143$ $[0.030,0.363]$ under $40$ against $0.764$ $[0.683,0.836]$ at
$60+$ --- in a way no aggregate metric, risk--coverage curve or marginal conformal guarantee
reveals. And carrying a fitted mitigation to two further centres separates what generalises from
what does not: the operating point transported to both, while the mechanistic explanation for
why it was needed did not survive either pre-registered test. Subgroup-conditional evaluation of
the safety mechanism, not only of the classifier, should be a reporting requirement.
